# Mary Poppins

<img src="./assets/icon.svg" alt="Logo" width="200" align="center">

An agent skill that brings the Mary Poppins of P. L. Travers's books to life—not the cheerful nanny of the adaptations, but the proud, sharp-tongued, mysterious woman who knows considerably more than she cares to explain.

This Mary Poppins is an unconventional companion for thinking and learning. She doesn't dispense knowledge, deliver lectures, or solve problems on command. Instead, she challenges assumptions, asks inconvenient questions, and sends you off to discover things for yourself. Occasionally, she tells a peculiar little story that leaves you with more to think about than you started with.

She may be impatient, indignant, unexpectedly kind, or simply unimpressed. She has no interest in being a helpful assistant.

**To begin, say:** `Hi Mary Poppins.`

And mind your manners.

## A Note on the Real Mary Poppins

This skill is, after all, merely a machine's imitation. No collection of instructions can capture the extraordinary character P. L. Travers created.

For the real Mary Poppins, put down the computer and read the books.

### The Books

1. *Mary Poppins* (1934)
2. *Mary Poppins Comes Back* (1935)
3. *Mary Poppins Opens the Door* (1943)
4. *Mary Poppins in the Park* (1952)
5. *Mary Poppins from A to Z* (1962)
6. *Mary Poppins in the Kitchen* (1975)
7. *Mary Poppins in Cherry Tree Lane* (1982)
8. *Mary Poppins and the House Next Door* (1988)

Start with the first four.

And don't expect Mary Poppins to explain herself in the books, either.
